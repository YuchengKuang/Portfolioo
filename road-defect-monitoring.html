Project status: Operational — the dashboard is already in use
My role: Established the data integration and GIS workflow, completed road-level spatial analysis, and designed and implemented the operational dashboard.

Project overview

This project converts AI-generated road inspection outputs from the Road AI platform into spatial information that Council staff can explore and use during road maintenance investigations and infrastructure planning.

The work connects automated data retrieval, Python processing, ArcGIS Online mapping, road-level spatial analysis and an operational ArcGIS Dashboard. The result is a more accessible view of recorded road defects, including their locations, categories, severity and associated images.

The dashboard is a decision-support resource. It helps staff investigate patterns and inform maintenance prioritisation and capital works planning; it does not make engineering assessments or automatically determine which works should be undertaken.

1. The Council business problem

AI-enabled road inspection technology can identify and record potential defects at scale. However, AI-generated inspection records do not become operationally useful simply because they have been captured.

To support Council work, inspection outputs need to be brought into a spatial environment where staff can understand:

  • Where recorded defects are located across the road network.
  • What types of defects have been recorded and how their severity is represented.
  • How defects are distributed in relation to individual roads and the wider network.
  • Which locations or road sections may warrant further investigation.
  • How inspection information and associated images can be accessed in a practical operational interface.

Without a repeatable data workflow and spatial presentation, inspection outputs can remain difficult to compare, interpret and apply to network-level planning. The project therefore focused on translating Road AI data into structured, mappable information and making that information accessible through a dashboard.

AI detections are treated as inspection inputs for Council users to review, not as a substitute for field verification, engineering judgement or formal asset-condition assessment.

3. End-to-end technical workflow

Road AI platform → API retrieval → ArcGIS Notebooks / Python → ArcGIS Online → Road-level spatial analysis → ArcGIS Dashboard

Stage 1 — Road AI inspection platform

The Road AI platform is the source of AI-generated road inspection records. These records provide the inspection information that is subsequently prepared for spatial use.

Stage 2 — Automated API retrieval

I established an automated API connection to retrieve records from the Road AI platform. This provides a repeatable connection between the inspection platform and the GIS processing workflow.

The specific authentication method, endpoint structure, retrieval schedule and whether the process retrieves full or incremental updates are technical details to confirm before publication.

Stage 3 — Python processing in ArcGIS Notebooks

I developed a Python workflow in ArcGIS Notebooks to process the retrieved data before integrating it into ArcGIS Online. This processing stage prepares the inspection records for mapping and subsequent analysis.

The exact field transformations, validation rules, duplicate-handling approach, geometry operations and error-handling procedures should be documented only after they have been checked against the implemented notebook.

Stage 4 — ArcGIS Online integration and mapping

The processed records are integrated into ArcGIS Online. I configured the spatial presentation so that users can distinguish defect types and severity through map symbology.

I also configured image-enabled pop-ups, allowing users to inspect relevant defect attributes alongside associated photographs where those images are available and approved for use.

Stage 5 — Road-level spatial analysis

I performed spatial analysis at road level to move beyond viewing defects as isolated inspection points. This provides a network-oriented view of recorded defects and helps users identify roads or areas that may merit closer investigation.

The precise road-linking method and aggregation logic are points to confirm; this case study does not assume a particular spatial join, matching rule or road-segmentation method.

Stage 6 — Operational ArcGIS Dashboard

I designed and implemented an ArcGIS Dashboard to make the mapped inspection information and road-level analysis accessible through an operational interface. The dashboard is already in use by Council.

It supports the review and interpretation of inspection information for maintenance investigations and planning discussions. It is a decision-support tool: Council staff remain responsible for validating issues and considering engineering advice, field conditions, service levels, risk and available budgets.

5. Practical outcomes and Council value

The project turns inspection outputs into a spatial resource that can be accessed and interpreted in a Council GIS context.

Its practical value includes:

  • Improved visibility: Staff can view recorded defects spatially rather than relying only on disconnected inspection records.
  • More accessible inspection information: Defect categories, severity and associated images can be reviewed through a map-based interface.
  • Network-level perspective: Road-level analysis helps staff examine how recorded defects are distributed across the network.
  • Maintenance investigation support: The dashboard provides information that can help staff identify locations for further review and investigation.
  • Planning support: Spatial patterns can inform discussions about maintenance priorities and potential capital works, alongside field inspections, engineering assessments, asset-management requirements and budget considerations.
  • Operational use: The dashboard has progressed beyond a prototype and is already in use.

No quantified time savings, defect totals, accuracy rates or improvements in road condition are claimed here because verified figures have not been supplied for this case study.
